01

Invoice settlement

MTInvoiceRegistry · arcpay.html

A service invoice becomes a link, the link becomes a QR code on the printed copy, and the customer pays it in one signature. USDC is Arc's native gas token, so there's no token approval and nothing to acquire first — they just pay. Settlement is final in under a second, and the contract never holds the money: every payment forwards to the treasury inside the same transaction.

When you'd use it

Tech finishes a no-cool call on a Saturday. Instead of leaving an invoice on the counter and chasing it for three weeks, the customer scans the QR before he's off the roof and it's settled — no card processor taking 2.9% + 30¢, no ACH wait, no "we pay on the 15th."

It's also the answer for a customer who's slow but not unwilling: the link works from a phone, at midnight, with no account to create.

02

Progress-billing escrow

MTProgressEscrow · arcdraw.html

The customer funds the whole job up front, so the money is provably there before equipment gets ordered. The contractor marks each draw complete, which starts an objection window; silence releases the draw automatically, an objection freezes it cold. There is no on-chain arbitrator, deliberately — a frozen draw stays frozen until a human relents, which makes stalemate visible instead of pretending code can settle a dispute.

When you'd use it — $38,000 RTU replacement

You don't want to order an $18,000 rooftop unit on your own credit. The customer doesn't want to hand $18,000 to a contractor before anything shows up. Normally whoever has more leverage wins that argument. Here the money is funded up front and visible to you, but locked until work stands behind each draw:

01Equipment ordered from the distributor$18,000
02Old unit down, curb adapter set, rigging complete$8,000
03New unit set, gas / electrical / controls connected$8,000
04Startup, commissioning, O&M turnover$4,000

You're not floating $18k on your own paper waiting for net-60. They're not paying for work that hasn't happened. If you walk, the long-stop returns their money without a lawyer.

Where it fits: new commercial customers, homeowners balking at a deposit, long-lead equipment. Where it doesn't: an FM platform with a PO system and net-60 terms — their AP process is the thing they're protecting.

03

MTYLD revenue vault

ERC-4626 · Mtyld.html

A USDC vault whose share price rises when Mechanical Temp reports revenue into it. Reporting mints no new shares, so nobody is diluted — the same claim is simply worth more. A permanently locked position was minted at launch and can never be redeemed by anyone, which keeps the share-price divisor from collapsing toward zero. That failure has bitten this builder before; the guard is the lesson.

Loading…

When you'd use it

A contractor's problem is that revenue arrives in lumps and expenses don't. The usual fix is a bank line of credit at 9% that wants a personal guarantee and two years of returns.

This is the other direction: holders put USDC in, the shop reports a slice of revenue back, and the share price rises for everyone at once. No new shares are minted, so a holder's stake is never diluted — the same claim is simply worth more. Mechanical Temp earns on the 1% exit fee rather than on a spread.

Why Arc

  • USDC as gasFees are dollar-denominated. A customer paying an invoice needs no second token and no bridge — the thing they're paying with is the thing that pays the fee.
  • Sub-secondMalachite consensus gives deterministic finality, not probabilistic confirmation. When a draw releases, it has released.
  • Real costEvery contract here was deployed, verified and exercised for roughly fifteen cents in total fees. A card processor would take about 75¢ on a single $25 invoice.
  • EVMStandard Solidity and standard tooling. Nothing here is exotic; that's the point.

What this isn't

Straight answers, because the alternative is embarrassing later.

This is testnet. Every dollar shown on these pages is play money from a faucet. Arc mainnet hasn't launched publicly, and none of this touches a real customer invoice yet.

The contracts are unaudited. They compile clean, they've been exercised end to end, and the vault's NAV math is verified against a reproducible simulation. That is not the same as a professional review, and the escrow and vault both hold funds.

No promises are attached to MTYLD. It is a claim on revenue that the operator chooses to report. It is not an offering, not a security pitch, and not a yield guarantee.
